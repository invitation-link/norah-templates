What to Write on a Housewarming Invitation

A housewarming invitation should say who is hosting, what you are celebrating, when guests should arrive, where the home is and how to RSVP. For Griha Pravesh, list the puja and meal times separately.

The essential details

  • Hosts' or family's names.
  • Housewarming, Griha Pravesh, house blessing or open house.
  • Date and day of the week.
  • Start time, plus puja and meal timings when relevant.
  • Full address and a map link.
  • RSVP contact or response request.
  • Dress, parking or no-gifts note only when useful.

Traditional Griha Pravesh wording

With gratitude and the blessings of our elders, we invite you and your family to our Griha Pravesh ceremony as we begin a new chapter in our home.

Puja: [Time] · Lunch: [Time]
Date: [Day, Date]
Venue: [Full Address]

Your presence and blessings will make our new beginning complete.

Warm, modern wording

We found the place, unpacked the boxes and saved the happiest part for you. Please join us for a housewarming at our new home on [Date] from [Time]. Come for a meal, a tour and the beginning of many memories here.

Short WhatsApp message

A new home deserves to be filled with familiar faces. We would love for you and your family to join our housewarming on [Date]. Open the invitation for timings, directions and RSVP: [Link]

House blessing wording

Please join us as we gather with gratitude to bless our new home on [Date] at [Time]. We would be honoured to begin this chapter with your prayers, presence and friendship.

How to say “no gifts” politely

Keep it gracious and secondary to the invitation. Try: “Your presence and blessings are all we need,” or “No gifts, please—come ready to share a meal and make a memory.”

Make the address easy to use

Write the complete address rather than relying only on a map pin. Add a landmark, building or flat number when it helps. Then include a direct Maps link and test it from a different phone before sharing.

A final proofreading checklist

  • Do the written date and day match?
  • Is it clear whether the puja time or meal time is the expected arrival?
  • Does the map open the correct entrance?
  • Is the host name and RSVP number correct?
  • Can an older guest read the details comfortably?
